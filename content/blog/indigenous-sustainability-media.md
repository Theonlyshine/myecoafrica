title: "Why Africa's Sustainability Story is Being Told by People Who Don't Live It"
tag: "Media & Communications"

summary: "The case for indigenous sustainability media — and what happens when the narrative is finally owned from the inside."
---

The dominant narrative around Africa's sustainability challenges is being produced, edited, and distributed by organisations headquartered outside the continent. This is not an accusation — it is a structural reality with measurable consequences.

When the people telling the story are not the people living it, specific things happen to the data: local complexity gets smoothed over, institutional nuance disappears, and the framing defaults to the frame of the funder rather than the frame of the community. The result is a body of sustainability media that is technically accurate but strategically incomplete.

This piece makes the case for indigenous sustainability media — not as a matter of cultural pride, but as a matter of data quality and decision-making accuracy. The institutions that need to act on Africa's sustainability transition need media that is honest about the complexity, not media that is designed to satisfy a donor reporting cycle.
